1. Percentages ARITHMETIC10 Qs in 2022–25

Formulas

  • Two successive changes a% and b%: net = a + b + ab/100. (+20 then −20 = −4%.)
  • If A is r% more than B, then B is r/(100+r) × 100% less than A. (25% more → 20% less.)
  • Price up r% → to keep spending the same, cut quantity by r/(100+r).
  • Growth over n periods: P(1 + r/100)ⁿ; overall from a% then b%: multiply factors (1.2 × 1.25 = 1.5).
  • "x% of y" = "y% of x" (8% of 50 = 50% of 8 = 4).

CAT 2024 S3Successive increase. After two raises, a salary is 187.5% of the original. The second raise (%) was twice the first. Find the first raise.

(1 + r)(1 + 2r) = 1.875. Try options: r = 0.25 → 1.25 × 1.5 = 1.875 ✓. 25%

CAT 2024 S1Share of total. Incomes 8 : 6 : 5. They pay 15%, 12%, 18% of income as rent. Next month the rent is the same and incomes rise 10%, 12%, 15%. What % of total income is rent now?

Take incomes 800, 600, 500. Rent = 120 + 72 + 90 = 282. New incomes = 880 + 672 + 575 = 2127. 282/2127 = 13.26%

CAT 2023 S3, 2024 S2Selling parts of a stock. Mangoes are 40% of the stock. He sells half the mangoes, 96 bananas and 40% of the apples, which is 50% of all fruit. Smallest possible stock?

Stock T, apples a: 0.2T + 96 + 0.4a = 0.5T → a = 0.75T − 240, bananas = 240 − 0.15T. Need a ≥ 1 (T > 320), all counts whole (T a multiple of 20). T = 340

CAT 2024 S3Overlap range (min/max). 250 students, girls 44%–60%. 50% of boys and 80% of girls swim; 70% of boys and 60% of girls run; everyone does at least one. Min and max doing both?

Both = swimmers + runners − total = 1.2(250 − g) + 1.4g − 250 = 50 + 0.2g. g from 110 to 150 → 72 and 80

CAT 2023 S3Down then up. Population 1,00,000 falls y% then rises x%, with x − y = 10 and the final above 1,00,000 (x, y whole). Lowest middle-year population?

(100 − y)(110 + y) > 10000 → y² + 10y − 1000 < 0 → y ≤ 27. Middle year = 73,000

CAT 2025 S1People leaving. More than 10 boys. 40% of girls and 60% of boys leave; the girls left are 8 more than the boys left. Minimum class size?

0.6g = 0.4b + 8 → 3g = 2b + 40, and b, g must be multiples of 5. b = 25, g = 30 → 55

2. Profit, loss and discount ARITHMETIC12 QsYOUR WEAK SPOT

Formulas

  • Profit % is on cost; discount % is on marked price. SP = CP(1 + p) = MP(1 − d).
  • MP/CP = (1 + p)/(1 − d). Example: 20% discount and 20% profit → MP = 1.5 × CP.
  • Same SP, one at +x% and one at −x% → overall a loss of x²/100 %.
  • "Buy n, get m free" → discount = m/(n + m). Giving 13 for the price of 12 → price per item × 12/13.
  • Cheating on weight: profit factor = (what you charge for)/(what you give). Combine factors by multiplying.

CAT 2024 S1Cost and price both change. Price set for 40% profit. Had cost been 40% less and price ₹5 less, profit would be 50%. Original price?

CP = c, SP = 1.4c. New: 1.4c − 5 = 1.5 × 0.6c = 0.9c → c = 10. SP = ₹14

CAT 2023 S1Equal selling prices. A and B sold at the same price: A at 20% profit, B at 10% loss. Both prices are raised (still equal) so B makes 10% profit. Profit on A now?

Price 1: CP_A = 1/1.2, CP_B = 1/0.9. New price = 1.1/0.9. Profit on A = (1.1/0.9) × 1.2 − 1 = 46.7% → ≈ 47%

CAT 2024 S3Discount saves an amount. Marked for 20% profit. A 10% discount saves the buyer ₹15. Seller's profit?

MP = 150, cost = 150/1.2 = 125, sold at 135 → ₹10

CAT 2025 S2Double the discount. Cost ₹1650, 20% profit after a discount. Doubling the discount leaves ₹110 profit. At what discount rate does profit % equal discount %?

SP 1980 and 1760 → M·d = 220, M = 2200. 2200(1 − x) = 1650(1 + x) → x ≈ 14.3% → 14

CAT 2025 S1Free items. 22% discount on MP and 13 chairs for the price of 12 still gives 26% profit. Cost ₹100 each. MP?

12 × 0.78M = 1.26 × 1300 → 9.36M = 1638 → M = 175

CAT 2023 S3Cheating on length. Buys at ₹100/m but gets 105 cm per metre. Sells at ₹110/m with a 5% discount, giving only 95 cm per metre. Profit?

Cost per real metre = 100/1.05. Revenue per real metre = 110 × 0.95/0.95 = 110. 110 × 1.05/100 − 1 = 15.5%

CAT 2024 S2Chain of sales. Bina loses 19% selling to Shyam at ₹4860. Selling at Hari's purchase price would have given her 17% profit. Shyam's profit?

Bina's cost = 4860/0.81 = 6000. Hari paid 6000 × 1.17 = 7020. Profit = 7020 − 4860 = ₹2160

3. Simple and compound interest ARITHMETIC11 Qs

Formulas

  • SI = P·r·t/100. Amount grows by the same amount each year.
  • CI amount = P(1 + r/100)ⁿ. Half-yearly: rate r/2, periods 2n. Quarterly: r/4, 4n.
  • Value(year 5)/Value(year 3) = (1 + r)². Successive CI amounts form a GP.
  • CI − SI for 2 years = P(r/100)².
  • Instalments: loan × (1+r)ⁿ = sum of each instalment grown to the end.

CAT 2023 S1, 2024 S2Compare two plans. Anil: ₹22,000 for 6 years at 4% p.a. compounded half-yearly. Sunil: same scheme for 5 years, then 10% simple interest for 1 year. Equal final amounts. Sunil's investment?

22000 × 1.02¹² = P × 1.02¹⁰ × 1.1 → P = 20000 × 1.02² = ₹20,808

CAT 2025 S1Find P and r from two SI amounts. ₹13,920 after 3 years and ₹18,960 after 6.5 years at SI. Interest if invested for 2 years compounded half-yearly?

3.5 years of interest = 5040 → 1440/year. P = 13920 − 4320 = 9600, r = 15%. 9600 × (1.075⁴ − 1) ≈ ₹3221

CAT 2024 S3Ratio of values. ₹4000 at CI. Value after 3 years : after 5 years = 25 : 36. Minimum years to exceed ₹20,000?

4. Ratio, proportion and variation ARITHMETIC10 QsYOUR WEAK SPOT

Formulas

  • Write a ratio a : b as ak, bk; then use the second condition to find k.
  • Moving x from A to B keeps the total fixed. Use the total as the anchor.
  • Combine A:B and B:C by making B equal (LCM).
  • Direct variation y = kx; with a square, y = kx². Find k from one data point.
  • Savings = income − expenditure. Write incomes and expenditures with separate multipliers if needed.

5. Mixtures and alligation ARITHMETIC12 Qs

Formulas

  • Alligation: mixing a cheaper c and a dearer d to get mean m: quantity ratio = (d − m) : (m − c).
  • Replacement: remove x from V and refill with the other liquid, n times. What remains of the original = V(1 − x/V)ⁿ.
  • Track the pure component (acid, milk). Adding water changes only the total.
  • Price of a mixture is a weighted average of the prices of its parts.

CAT 2024 S1Repeated replacement. A glass of milk; two-thirds poured out and replaced with water, four times in all. Milk : water?

Milk left = (1/3)⁴ = 1/81 → 1 : 80

CAT 2023 S2How many replacements. 40 L milk; replace 4 L with water each time. Smallest number of times until milk < water?

40 × 0.9ⁿ < 20 → 0.9ⁿ < 0.5. 0.9⁶ ≈ 0.53, 0.9⁷ ≈ 0.48 → 7

CAT 2023 S1Two replacements, ratio given. Coffee replaced twice by cocoa; final coffee : cocoa = 16 : 9. Cocoa in first mix : second mix?

(1 − f)² = 16/25 → f = 1/5. Cocoa 1/5 then 9/25 → 5 : 9

CAT 2024 S2Add water, then acid. Add 2 L water → 50% acid. Then add 15 L acid → 80% acid. Original water : acid?

a = w + 2 and a + 15 = 0.8(a + w + 17) → a + 7 = 4w. w = 3, a = 5 → 3 : 5

6. Averages ARITHMETIC17 Qs: the most common arithmetic topicYOUR WEAK SPOT

Formulas

  • Sum = average × count. Work with sums, not averages.
  • A newcomer changes the average by (newcomer − old average)/(new count).
  • Weighted average of groups = Σ(count × avg)/Σcount. Overlapping groups: add the sums and subtract the overlap.
  • Average of an AP = (first + last)/2.

Formulas

  • Take the total work as the LCM of the times. Rate = work/time. Rates add; a drain pipe is negative.
  • Efficiency ratio a : b means time ratio b : a.
  • Man-hours: M₁D₁H₁/W₁ = M₂D₂H₂/W₂.

RC: how to read 16 Qs

Reading method (about 3 minutes for a 450-word passage)

  1. Read the first paragraph slowly. It usually sets up the topic and the debate.
  2. After each paragraph, say its job in 3–5 words in your head: "problem", "old view", "author disagrees", "example", "solution".
  3. Mark signal words: however, yet, but (turn), therefore, thus (conclusion), for instance (example), some argue (a view the author may reject).
  4. Before the questions, answer two things: What is the author's main point? and What is the author's attitude? (critical, supportive, balanced, sceptical).
  5. For every question, go back to the line. Never answer from memory of the passage.

RC question types

TypeHow it is askedHow to answer
Main idea / purpose / title"The passage is primarily concerned with", "best title"The answer covers the whole passage, including the turn. Reject options that cover one paragraph (too narrow) or the whole field (too broad).

The six traps that took your marks this week

  1. Mentioned, wrong role. Solar power appeared in the passage as something the oil lobby sabotages, so it was not a reason policies fail.
  2. True in the world, not in the passage. If the passage doesn't say it, don't pick it, however sensible.
  3. Extreme words: always, never, all, only, entirely, deified, proves. Rarely right.
  4. Half right. The first half matches the passage, the second half adds something new. One wrong part makes the option wrong.
  5. Reversed cause and effect. "A causes B" turned into "B causes A".
  6. Familiar words, different meaning. An option that copies phrases from the passage but rearranges them into a claim the author didn't make.

Para-jumbles (TITA, no negative)

Method: lock pairs first, then the opener

  1. Find mandatory pairs. A sentence that starts with This, These, Such, It, They, However, But, Yet, Also, For example, As a result, Instead must follow something specific. Find that something.
  2. Full name before short name. "The Unified Payments Interface" comes before "UPI" or "the system".
  3. Opener: introduces the subject by full name or makes a general statement, and has no unresolved pronoun or connector.
  4. Closer: a conclusion (thus, therefore, in this sense) or a forward-looking line.
  5. Chronology: older before newer (in the 1960s… today).
  6. Write the chain, then read it once in order. If any link jars, swap the doubtful sentence and re-read.
  7. Always enter an answer. Even a partly right order scores nothing extra, but a blank is the same zero.

Method: build the four-sentence paragraph

Para summary (MCQ, −1 if wrong)

Method: main point + its limit

  1. Read the paragraph and find the last claim; that is usually the point. Earlier sentences are evidence or background.
  2. Write your own one-line summary before reading the options. Include any condition the author attaches ("depends on", "only when", "in part").
  3. Eliminate: too narrow (one detail), too broad (a bigger claim), extreme (stronger than the text), new information or advice the author never gave, distortion (cause turned around).
  4. Between two close options, pick the one that keeps the author's contrast or condition.

Method

DILR

About 22 questions in 4–5 sets, 40 minutes. Sets have 4–6 questions each, and several are TITA. Your score depends far more on which sets you pick than on speed. Two sets fully solved (8–10 correct, roughly 24–30 marks) is a strong DILR score in recent years.

Section plan: pick, then commit

The first 4 minutes decide the section

  1. Scan every set for 40–50 seconds. Read only the first paragraph and the questions. Don't solve anything yet.
  2. Rank them using the table below. Prefer sets with fixed numbers and few variables, questions that look direct ("how many", "who"), and a clear table you can draw.

Set types, how to spot them, how to set up

The types below come from CAT papers 2017–2025. Each row: what it looks like, the grid to draw, and the trick that usually unlocks it.

1. Linear and circular arrangement

Signal: people in a row, around a table, on floors of a building, in a queue.
Setup: draw the slots (numbered positions or a circle). Place the person with the most fixed condition first.
Trick: for a circle, fix one person's seat; everything else is relative. "Opposite" only works with an even number of seats. Write two or three cases side by side when one condition splits; most cases die after one or two more rules.

2. Distribution and matching (grid)

Signal: each person has one item from each of several categories (city, job, colour).
Setup: a person × attribute table. Use ✓ and ✗ in a person × option grid when there are many negative clues.
Trick: a row or column with all ✗ except one forces that cell. Re-scan the clues after each new ✓.

3. Scheduling and calendars

Signal: meetings, exams, shifts, slots across days or hours.
Setup: a day × slot grid. Mark blocked slots first.
Trick: look for an event with the fewest possible slots. "No two consecutive days" and "before/after" rules drastically cut the options.

4. Tournaments

Signal: round-robin (everyone plays everyone) or knockout.
Formulas: round-robin with n teams: n(n−1)/2 matches. Knockout: n−1 matches to find a winner. With 3 points for a win and 1 each for a draw, a decisive match adds 3 points and a draw adds 2, so total points = 3 × matches − draws.
Trick: use the total points to find the number of draws. A results matrix (team × team) shows W/L/D at a glance.

5. Games, scoring and rounds

Signal: players earn points across rounds, with rules for passing, elimination or bonuses.
Setup: player × round table, with a running total column.
Trick: work backwards from the final scores; the possible combinations of round scores are usually very few.

6. Venn diagrams and sets

Signal: people who like A, B, C; "only", "exactly two", "at least one".
Formula: n(A∪B∪C) = A + B + C − (AB + BC + CA) + ABC. "Exactly two" = sum of pair overlaps − 3·ABC.
Trick: label all 7 regions with variables (a–g), write the given equations, and solve from the centre (ABC) outwards. For max/min questions, push the free variable to its limit.

7. Tables with missing data

Signal: a table with some cells blank, plus row/column totals or averages.
Trick: fill the row or column with only one blank first. Totals = average × count. Usually the most scoring set type in the paper; pick it if you see it.

8. Charts and data interpretation

Signal: bar, line, pie or scatter charts, often with percentages.
Trick: read the axis labels and units first. Approximate (percentages to the nearest 5%) unless options are close. Use the calculator only for the final step. Watch for "percentage points" vs "percent" and for questions asking about a change versus a level.

9. Ranking, order and comparison

Signal: who is taller, faster, ranked higher; positions in several lists.
Setup: a vertical chain with > signs. Branches show uncertainty.
Trick: count how many people are definitely above or below someone to answer "how many possible ranks".

10. Routes, networks and flows

Signal: cities connected by roads, pipes, flights, a maze; costs or capacities on edges.
Setup: draw the network with weights.
Trick: in a flow, what goes into a node equals what goes out. For shortest path, list paths from the start in order of cost. Very few nodes ever matter.

11. Quant-based LR (ratings, averages, investments)

Signal: scores given by judges, averages after removing the highest and lowest, money invested in projects, shares or auctions.
Trick: translate each rule into an equation or an inequality. These are arithmetic in disguise, so your Quant formulas (averages, percentages, ratios) do the work. Integers and "distinct values" are strong constraints.

12. Selection and committees with constraints

Signal: choose k people from a group; "if A is chosen, B must be", "C and D can't be together".
Trick: write each rule as an arrow (A → B, C ✗ D). Use the contrapositive (not B → not A). Start from the most restrictive person.

13. Truth-tellers and liars, claims

Signal: people always tell the truth, always lie, or alternate; statements about each other.
Trick: assume one person is truthful and follow the chain. A contradiction kills the case. Two statements that contradict each other mean exactly one of the speakers is lying.

14. Grids, maps, coordinates and processes

Signal: a city grid, a board, a robot moving step by step, a queue or a machine process.
Trick: simulate the first few steps on paper and look for a cycle or pattern. Use coordinates for grids; distances are often |Δx| + |Δy| (Manhattan distance).

General rules

  1. Never read the whole set twice. Read once while drawing the grid. Then go clue by clue.
  2. Write cases openly (Case 1, Case 2). Don't hold them in your head.
  3. Questions often give you extra information. Use it only for that question, then go back to the base grid.
  4. A question you can answer from a partly solved grid: answer it now. Many sets have 2 easy questions even if you can't finish.
  5. "Which of these must be true" = true in every case. "Can be true" = true in at least one case.
  6. Check your units and totals once before typing a TITA.
  7. If you've spent 15 minutes with fewer than 2 correct answers in a set, leave it.
